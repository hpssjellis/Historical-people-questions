
## English


| Activity # | 1. Basic Brain Ability (The Internal Particle) | 2. LLM Analysis (The External Wave) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1: Vocabulary** | **Etymological Roots Tracing:** Students are given a single Latin or Greek root (e.g., *spec*, *mal*, *logy*) and must mentally generate as many related English words as possible in 60 seconds, connecting sound to meaning. | **Synonym Context Swap:** Prompt the LLM to rewrite a famous paragraph using a list of five synonyms for key words. Students then analyze the LLM's choices and argue *which* synonym was contextually inappropriate, proving the machine lacks true semantic nuance. |
| **2: Structure** | **Thesis-to-Outline Race:** Give students a complex thesis statement. They must race against the clock to draft a 5-point, logically sequenced, argumentative outline (I, II, III, IV, V) using only Roman numerals and topic phrases, forcing immediate logical organization. | **Argument Structure Critique:** Use the LLM to generate a five-paragraph persuasive essay on a controversial topic. Students use color-coding to map the essay's structure (Thesis, Topic Sentences, Evidence, Counter-Argument, Conclusion), identifying structural weaknesses or illogical paragraph breaks. |
| **3: Syntax** | **Punctuation Puzzles:** Give students a paragraph written entirely without commas, semicolons, or apostrophes. They must rewrite the passage, using only their knowledge of grammatical rules to structure meaning and clarify complex clauses. | **The Run-on Challenge:** Prompt the LLM to combine 10 short, simple sentences into one grammatically correct *and* stylistically effective complex sentence. Students analyze the machine's syntax, judging whether it created an elegant periodic sentence or an ugly, confusing run-on. |
| **4: Poetry** | **Rhyme and Meter Guessing:** Read an unfamiliar sonnet aloud and pause every two lines. Students must predict the next rhyming word based on meter and context, reinforcing the *aural* structure of language. | **Poetry Parody/Manipulation:** Input a well-known poem (e.g., Shakespeare, Dickinson) and prompt the LLM to rewrite it in the style of a modern pop song lyric or a corporate memo. Students then compare the *artistic loss* in translation, defining what makes the original form sacred. |
| **5: Narrative** | **Character Arc Mapping (Memory):** After reading a text, students close their books and, from memory, quickly draw a visual plot line showing the emotional/psychological trajectory (the arc) of a main character, focusing on causality. | **Alternate Ending Auditing:** Prompt the LLM to generate three different plausible alternate endings for a novel studied in class. Students must debate which ending is *most* consistent with the author's themes and character motivations, justifying their choice using textual evidence the LLM cannot access. |
| **6: Figurative Language**| **Metaphor Generation Drill:** Give students two disparate objects (e.g., *a highway* and *ambition*). They must generate three original metaphors or similes comparing the two concepts in under one minute. | **Cliché Detector:** Prompt the LLM to write a descriptive paragraph using as many common literary clichés as possible. Students identify and replace every cliché with an original, vivid, human-generated piece of figurative language. |
| **7: Research** | **Citation Sequencing (APA/MLA):** Give students the raw components of a citation (Author, Title, Year, Publisher) and have them mentally order the elements correctly for both MLA and APA formats, reinforcing procedural memory. | **Source Verification Scavenger Hunt:** Have the LLM summarize a historical event (e.g., the Cuban Missile Crisis) and include six distinct citations (some real, some fabricated "hallucinations"). Students must conduct a rapid *scavenger hunt* using search tools to verify the legitimacy of every single source. |
| **8: Dialect/Tone** | **Tone Shifting Rewrite:** Present a formal letter requesting a raise. Students must manually rewrite the letter for three different recipients (a best friend, a strict boss, a parent), mastering rapid shifts in *diction and tone*. | **AI Voice Mimicry:** Prompt the LLM to rewrite a personal journal entry in a highly formal, detached, and academic "LLM voice." Students analyze the rewriting to pinpoint where the human element (emotion, vulnerability, specific memory) was sterilized by the AI's neutrality. |
| **9: Editing** | **"The Red Pen" Silent Correction:** Students swap a short essay and spend 10 minutes silently proofreading and editing their partner's work using a red pen, forcing deep, concentrated attention to detail and error detection. | **Automated Grammar Check Critique:** Students use a standard grammar checker (like the one built into word processing software or a basic LLM) on their finished paper. They must *reject* at least 25% of the AI's suggestions, justifying why the original phrasing was stylistically superior or grammatically valid. |
| **10: Critical Theory** | **Conceptual Definition Quiz:** Give students rapid-fire terms from literary theory (e.g., *Post-structuralism, Hegemony, Absurdism*). They must provide a concise, orally-delivered definition from memory, building the conceptual framework. | **Theory Application Test:** Give the LLM a novel and ask it to write a paragraph analyzing it using *Post-Colonial Theory*. Students must critique the LLM's application, pointing out where it missed key nuances or simply inserted buzzwords without genuine theoretical insight. |
