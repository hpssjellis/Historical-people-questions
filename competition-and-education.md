Conclusion on Awards
We should absolutely remove class- and school-wide awards that identify one student as better than another based on academic output (e.g., Valedictorian for GPA). This is a trivial exercise in the age of perfect AI performance.

We should replace them with non-competitive Honors and Citations that celebrate the irreplaceable human process:

Honors should be granted based on a student crossing a specific, self-defined threshold of virtuous behavior (e.g., achieving the Autonomy Award by completing the IH-assessment cycle).

Voluntary competitions (like Ethics Bowls or Debate) are excellent because they reward the Aristotelian virtues of practical wisdom, courage, and intellectual skill in a context where the rules of the game are transparent and the competition is sought by the student.

The debate is not about removing fun, but about ensuring the competition and rewards are focused on what it means to be an excellent human, not an excellent data processor.
